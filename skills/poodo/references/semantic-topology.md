# Semantic topology traversal

Treat the user's stated goal as a constraint surface—a hyperplane in the working metaphor—surrounded by a connected topology of meanings, mechanisms, evidence, dependencies, actors, risks, and possible interventions. POODO must explore the decision-relevant neighborhood before collapsing onto a resolution, solution, or output.

## Construct the map

Create a compact topology map whose nodes and links cover, as relevant:

- user terms, alternate meanings, hidden definitions, and neighboring formulations;
- desired outcomes, anti-goals, acceptance surfaces, stakeholders, and incentives;
- mechanisms, causal chains, feedback loops, lifecycle states, and temporal horizons;
- components, dependencies, interfaces, versions, environments, and authority boundaries;
- evidence families, sensors, oracles, missing observations, and provenance lineage;
- known failure modes, edge cases, contradictions, rival frames, and unknown unknown probes;
- solution families, experiments, containment, rollback, no-action, and escalation paths.

Label each region by epistemic state and relevance. Links should expose how changing one interpretation, dependency, assumption, or intervention affects other regions. Preserve discontinuities: two similar words or symptoms may belong to different mechanisms.

## Traverse the neighborhood

Traverse breadth before depth, then deepen load-bearing and high-uncertainty regions. Use operations such as semantic expansion/contraction, causal upstream/downstream tracing, stakeholder rotation, lifecycle traversal, dependency/interface walking, counterexample search, boundary-value analysis, inversion, analogy with applicability checks, and rival-hypothesis testing.

Use fan-out workers to explore genuinely different regions, evidence families, or traversal methods. Reconcile overlaps by evidence lineage rather than votes. When new evidence opens a material region, add it to the map and revisit any convergence it destabilizes.

## Bounded saturation gate

Literal exhaustive traversal is generally unknowable. Before collapsing, justify bounded saturation:

1. Every decision-relevant region in the current map is explored, explicitly out of scope, inaccessible, or carried forward as unresolved.
2. Every load-bearing assumption has supporting evidence, a planned discriminator, or an uncertainty label that constrains the decision.
3. At least one rival traversal challenges the favored frame from a different mechanism, evidence family, stakeholder, or boundary condition.
4. Additional passes, research, and fan-out yield no material new region or no plausible decision-changing information within the authorized budget.
5. The 20 paths span materially different topology regions rather than clustering around the first solution.
6. The collapse preserves reversal conditions, uncovered regions, discarded alternatives with reasons, and triggers for reopening the map.

Record the explored regions, traversal methods, new nodes/links, unresolved frontier, saturation rationale, and convergence boundary. Never translate “fully traversed” into an unqualified completeness claim; state the named scope within which coverage is sufficient for the next decision.
