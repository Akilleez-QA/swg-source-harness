# Observe operation

Establish current state through direct inspection, measurement, comparison, boundary mapping, or focused source review.

Build a provisional topology map around the stated goal as observations accumulate. Capture relevant terms and interpretations, mechanisms, entities and stakeholders, dependencies and interfaces, evidence families, temporal and lifecycle states, failure modes, neighboring problem formulations, solution families, constraints, and boundary conditions. Mark regions as observed, inferred, reported, unknown, inaccessible, or out of scope. Use the map to find blind spots; do not treat it as evidence by itself.

For each load-bearing observation record source type, stable locator, time or artifact identity, freshness, evidence family, scope, and non-entailments. Distinguish user-observed surfaces, reports about external facts, tool output, source claims, and inference. Missing evidence is not evidence of absence.

## Mandatory deep-web semantic-expansion gate

This is the final operation of Observe and a hard prerequisite for Orient. Perform it on every POODO loop, including resumed and post-contradiction loops. Its purpose is to enlarge and challenge the model's semantic frame before synthesis, not to collect citations that decorate an existing conclusion.

1. Translate the observed problem and provisional topology map into research questions covering the domain mechanism, governing specification or established convention, terminology and alternate formulations, dependencies, failure modes, adjacent solution families, boundary conditions, and the strongest credible rival explanation.
2. Search the web deeply enough to encounter materially different evidence families. Prefer primary sources: official specifications and documentation, original research, standards, maintainers' source or issue records, and authoritative institutional guidance. Use high-quality secondary sources only to locate, contextualize, or challenge primary evidence.
3. Include explicit disconfirmation searches aimed at finding conditions under which the favored interpretation fails. Do not formulate every query to presuppose the current hypothesis.
4. Triangulate load-bearing claims across at least two meaningfully independent sources when such sources exist. Multiple pages repeating one upstream statement are one evidence family.
5. Inspect the relevant source content rather than relying on search-result snippets. Record publication/update date, retrieval date, stable URL, authority, scope, conflicts, and what the source does not establish for the current artifact or environment.
6. Protect scope and privacy. Search generalized technical or conceptual descriptions when local names, unpublished code, credentials, personal data, or proprietary details are unnecessary. Web research never authorizes disclosure.
7. Produce a compact research record before Orient: questions and queries used; sources and evidence families; semantic additions or changed terminology; contradictions and unresolved uncertainty; applicability to the observed case; and at least one implication that could alter a hypothesis, test, or decision.

Treat research as semantic context, not an epistemic trump card. For deep technical problems, public results may be incomplete, outdated, copied without verification, written for another version, or absent for proprietary and poorly documented systems. Rank evidence by its fitness for the exact claim. Exact-version specifications and source, direct observation of the bound artifact, reproducible experiments, and known-working local controls normally outweigh generic articles, forum consensus, search ranking, or documentation for a different release. A web source may introduce terminology, mechanisms, failure modes, or test ideas without establishing that any of them apply locally. Preserve conflicts for Orient; do not discard strong local evidence to make the research narrative coherent, and do not reject useful web context merely because it is not dispositive.

The gate is not satisfied by prior model knowledge, repository-only inspection, one source, uninspected snippets, citation count, or sources selected only because they agree. If network access, browsing capability, or minimally adequate sources are unavailable, mark the gate `unsatisfied`, state what was attempted and what is missing, and stop before Orient. Do not simulate research or proceed by relabeling recollection as evidence.
